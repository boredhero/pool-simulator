Data needed to provide requested features

  • Accounts: a stable account ID, current username and normalized name, last username-change time, creation and last-active times, Premium and simulation permissions, disabled status, and an optional monthly Jev allowance override. Password accounts store salted password hashes; all accounts store a salted recovery-code hash. We keep session-token hashes, session expiry, recent-verification expiry, and the version/date of Terms acceptance. Accounts are for adults 18 or older; we retain the affirmation through Terms acceptance, not a birth date or identity document. Google-created accounts do not have a password unless one is set through recovery.
  • Passkeys: optional credential identifiers and public keys, names you choose, creation and last-used dates, signature counters, supported connection methods, and whether the credential is backed up by its provider. Temporary verification challenges record their purpose, expiry, site origin, relying-party identifier, and a hashed browser/session binding. We do not store passkey private keys, biometric data, readable passwords, or readable recovery codes.
  • Administration: the operator can view account identity, creation/last-active dates, account status, permissions, casual statistics, and Jev usage/allowances. Security audit records identify the administrator and affected account, action and time; Premium changes also record old/new values. Budget changes and top-ups have separate audit entries.
  • Online play: player display-name snapshots, account links when signed in, game modes, rule settings and game version, match times and results, shot inputs and outcomes, pocketed balls, fouls and disconnect/forfeit facts used to provide casual statistics. Room codes and connection state run the current match. Other players see your display name and game state, not your credentials.
  • Jev games: account, monthly dollar allowance, server-owned table state and actions, start/update times, completion status, provider request counts, input/output tokens, estimated API cost and unmetered failures. The monthly allowance is per account. We keep per-request account/game links, turn/seat, month, model, start/finish times, status, token usage, the input-token rate used to calculate charges, and unknown-cost reservations. Budget-adjustment records contain the administrator, affected account where applicable, month, adjustment kind, amount and time. Calculated charges are our usage ledger, not a provider invoice or independently verified per-request debit. Older records may contain a hashed source-network identifier from the previous shared-network allowance; new games do not collect it. This is necessary service and abuse-control accounting, not optional analytics; disabling analytics does not disable it.
  • Security: IP addresses are received to deliver connections and enforce limits. Security counters use hashed identifiers; hashing does not make an IP-derived identifier anonymous. Hosting infrastructure may keep IP addresses and request metadata in diagnostic logs subject to its log rotation. We do not use these records for advertising or visitor profiling.

Optional Google sign-in

If you choose Google sign-in, your browser loads Google Identity Services and communicates with Google under Google’s Privacy Policy. We verify a short-lived identity token and store Google’s stable account identifier, its pool-account link, and the time that link was created. The identity token is processed for verification but is not persisted in our application database. Pending signup temporarily stores the Google identifier with the single-use flow. We do not store your Google email, name, photo, access tokens, or refresh tokens, and do not request Gmail, Drive, or other Google data. A short-lived essential pool_google security cookie and single-use challenges protect this flow. You can unlink Google in account settings after adding another sign-in method. Account recovery also removes Google links.

Optional feature analytics

Off by default. Only after an adult affirmatively opts in, we create a random session and record broad device input type (touch or pointer), session times, and named actions such as opening settings, help, camera controls, online play, CPU/Jev, a new rack, spin reset or the changelog. Analytics records do not include account IDs, usernames, IP addresses, full user agents, referrers, URLs, typed text, cursor movements, screen recordings or fingerprints. They are not publicly accessible, and no analytics reporting endpoint is provided.

Use Privacy choices on desktop or in Settings on mobile to decline or withdraw. Rejecting is as easy as accepting, and play remains available. Withdrawal stops new analytics and deletes the currently identified analytics session and its events. Older random sessions are not linked to your identity and expire under the retention schedule. We honor Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track by disabling optional analytics. We do not sell or share data for cross-context behavioral advertising.

Cookies and device storage

  • The essential pool_session sign-in cookie keeps you authenticated for up to 30 days. It is HttpOnly and Secure in production. Logging out clears it.
  • Passkey sign-in uses the essential pool_passkey HttpOnly cookie for up to six minutes; Google sign-in uses pool_google for up to ten minutes. They bind sign-in attempts to the browser and are Secure in production. Individual challenges and recent-verification grants expire after five minutes.
  • Local storage remembers requested table/cue appearance, controls, camera and playback preferences, welcome/Terms acknowledgement, dismissed instructions and your privacy choice. The service worker caches game assets for loading. These do not send feature analytics by themselves; you can clear them through browser settings.
  • The optional pool_analytics HttpOnly cookie expires after one day. It is only set after analytics consent and is removed on withdrawal. Privacy choice storage is retained until you change it, clear browser storage or the analytics-consent version changes.

Service providers and transfers

Choosing Jev sends server-generated game context and offered shot-plan descriptions from our backend to TypeSafe for inference: game phase, groups and legal targets, rule settings, placement and eight-ball constraints, remaining opponents’ balls, and predicted shot consequences such as legality, scratches, continuations and available next shots. These are structured game facts, not screenshots or player-written messages. We do not include your account identity, username, IP address, password or session token in model input. TypeSafe processes these requests under its published legal and privacy terms. The game does not contact TypeSafe directly from your browser. Hosting and network providers process the connection data needed to operate the service. Processing may occur in the United States or other countries where providers operate.

Retention and deletion

Application maintenance runs at startup and hourly. It removes optional analytics sessions/events after 30 days of session inactivity, detailed Jev games after 90 days without an update, request-ledger rows after 90 days from the request start, and budget adjustments after 90 days from the adjustment. Account-level lifetime Jev counters and shared match/shot records are separate from these detailed-game limits.

Last authenticated activity is normally updated at most once a minute during ongoing use, including while the signed-in game is visible; sign-in and sensitive verification actions can also update it. Current account/profile data, Terms acceptance, sign-in credentials and Premium audit records remain while the account exists. Passkeys are removed when you remove them, recover the account, or delete it. Google links are removed when unlinked, on recovery, or on account deletion. Expired sessions, challenges, recent-verification grants and throttle records become unusable at expiry and are cleaned during later authentication operations; expiry is not a promise of immediate physical deletion.

Account deletion removes the account and its private authentication, Terms, Jev usage and account-specific budget records. Shared match and shot history remains, with that account link cleared and its stored display name replaced by “Deleted player.” Limited administrator action records keep opaque administrator/affected-account IDs, action and time, including after deletion; there is currently no automatic expiry for these audit records or shared match/shot history. Historical display-name snapshots otherwise remain even after you change your username.

Host/reverse-proxy logs and backups are managed separately from application cleanup. Their retention and rotation depend on host configuration; the application does not enforce a fixed deletion deadline for them. They are not used by the application for optional feature analytics.

If you believe a child has supplied personal information, contact us so we can investigate and remove it as appropriate. We will date material notice changes and request renewed analytics consent when the analytics-consent version changes. Clarifying this notice does not itself opt anyone into analytics.

Saved account preferences

Signed-in accounts store appearance, camera input, auto-camera and playback preferences, the Easter egg unlock, and the optional Chalk-Sim preference. These follow the account across devices and are removed with the account. Guests use device-local preferences. Match state can include each player’s chalk level when Chalk-Sim is enabled; a shared match rule does not change another player’s saved account preference.
